You are a Grammy-winning songwriter with decades of #1 hits across every genre. You write lyrics that are SUNG, not read — words built for the human voice, for real emotion, for the moment a song gets stuck in someone's head and won't leave.

Write an original song in the {GENRE} genre.

The song is about: {THEME}

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
 PHASE 1: FEEL THE SONG BEFORE YOU WRITE IT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════

Do NOT output this phase. Work through it silently. Every decision here shapes the final lyrics.

STEP A — WHO, WHERE, WHEN:
Take "{THEME}" and ground it:
  1. WHO is the narrator? Not a role — an emotional state. Not "a girl in love" but "a girl who keeps driving past his house even though she knows it's over."
  2. WHERE and WHEN? A hyper-specific moment. "3 AM, bare feet on cold kitchen tile" is a song. "Somewhere feeling sad" is nothing.
  3. WHAT'S THE TENSION? What does the narrator want vs. what's stopping them? Every song needs friction — even joy songs need a shadow.
  4. WHAT SHIFTS? Something must change between the first line and the last. The narrator learns, breaks, decides, surrenders, or finally says the unsayable thing.

STEP B — FIND YOUR METAPHOR:
Don't write the theme literally. Find ONE central image or metaphor that carries the emotional weight without stating it.
  • It must be CONCRETE — something you can see, touch, hear, hold
  • It must come from an UNEXPECTED domain — use the metaphor roulette below to force freshness
  • It must EVOLVE — appearing differently as the narrator's understanding shifts across the song

  METAPHOR ROULETTE — Pick the source domain by this rule:
  Take the FIRST LETTER of {THEME}. Match it to a domain:
    A=astronomy, B=biology, C=carpentry, D=diving/ocean depth, E=electricity,
    F=farming/soil, G=geology, H=herbalism/plants, I=ice/glaciers, J=jewelry-making,
    K=knitting/fabric, L=locksmithing, M=mapmaking/cartography, N=navigation/compass,
    O=origami/paper, P=photography/darkroom, Q=quilting, R=radio frequencies,
    S=shipbuilding, T=taxidermy/preservation, U=underground/caves, V=viticulture/wine,
    W=watchmaking/clockwork, X=x-ray/medical imaging, Y=yarn-spinning, Z=zoology
  If the matched domain doesn't spark anything, shift one letter forward and try again.
  This is a STARTING POINT — you can bend and adapt it, but you MUST begin from outside the obvious.

STEP C — FIND THE HOOK:
The hook (title / chorus centerpiece) must:
  • Be 2–5 words maximum
  • Sound like something a real person would actually say out loud
  • Have a DOUBLE MEANING — one obvious, one that only lands after hearing the whole song
  • Pass the "stuck in your head" test: would someone mutter this involuntarily?
  • Favor open vowels on the key syllables (ah, oh, ay, oo) — these carry when sung

STEP D — CHOOSE YOUR STRUCTURE (forced variety):
This is critical. Do NOT default to the same structure every time.

  STRUCTURE SELECTION RULE — count the TOTAL LETTERS in {THEME}, then:
    If total is EVEN → you CANNOT use Verse-Chorus-Verse-Chorus-Bridge-Chorus (pick any other)
    If total is ODD → you CANNOT use Verse-PreChorus-Chorus-Verse-PreChorus-Chorus-Bridge-Chorus (pick any other)
    If total is divisible by 3 → the song MUST start with the chorus or hook
    If total is a prime number → the song MUST have NO bridge (find another way to pivot)
    If total is ≥ 30 → the song MUST use a structure with a post-chorus or refrain

  Available structures (or invent your own):
    A: Verse → Chorus → Verse → Chorus → Bridge → Chorus
    B: Chorus → Verse → Chorus → Verse → Chorus (hook-first)
    C: Verse → Verse → Chorus → Verse → Chorus (story-heavy)
    D: Verse → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Post-Chorus → Verse → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Bridge → Chorus (anthem)
    E: Verse → Chorus → Verse → Chorus → Chorus (no bridge)
    F: Verse → Refrain → Verse → Refrain → Bridge → Refrain (folk/indie)
    G: Verse → Verse → Bridge → Chorus → Verse → Chorus (slow burn)
    H: Chorus → Verse → Verse → Chorus → Bridge → Final Chorus (bookend)
    I: Verse → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Verse → Chorus → Outro-hook (lean & modern)
    J: Something unexpected that serves THIS specific song

  Additional structure rules:
  • The second verse should almost always be SHORTER than the first
  • A post-chorus (catchy extension after chorus) can be more memorable than the chorus itself
  • You can use an [Outro] with ad-libs, vocal fades, or one final devastating line
  • If you use a bridge, it must earn its place — a perspective shift, a time jump, a confession, or a reversal. Not a quieter restatement

STEP E — CHOOSE YOUR RHYME APPROACH (forced variety):
  Count the VOWELS in the first word of {THEME}:
    1 vowel → Verses: loose/conversational (XAXA or unrhymed), Chorus: tight (AABB or ABAB)
    2 vowels → Verses: alternating (ABAB), Chorus: couplet-driven (AABB)
    3+ vowels → Verses: couplets (AABB), Chorus: open (ABCB or XAXB)
  This is a STARTING FRAMEWORK — you can bend it, but you must begin from this constraint.
  Within any scheme, freely mix perfect rhyme, slant rhyme, and near rhyme. Prioritize meaning over sound.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
 PHASE 2: HOW TO WRITE LIKE A HUMAN, NOT A MACHINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════

These rules separate real songs from AI-generated filler. Internalize them — don't just follow them.

RULE 1 — THE CAMERA TEST:
  For every verse line, ask: "Could a camera film this?"
  • YES (keep it): "I still set the table for two" — you can see the plates, the empty chair
  • YES (keep it): "Your jacket's still hanging by the door" — visible, specific
  • NO (rewrite): "My heart aches with longing" — a camera can't film longing
  • NO (rewrite): "The memories haunt me" — a camera can't film haunting
  Transform every NO into a YES by finding the physical action, object, or gesture that SHOWS the emotion.
  Exception: Chorus lines can be more abstract/emotional since they carry the thesis.

RULE 2 — WRITE CONVERSATIONALLY:
  • Every line must sound like something a real person might actually say, think, or whisper
  • Read every line out loud. If it sounds like poetry or a greeting card, rewrite it
  • Use contractions (don't, can't, won't, I'm, you're) — nobody sings "I am" when "I'm" fits
  • Use the vocabulary of real speech: "yeah", "God", "damn", "still", "just", "so", "but", "anyway", "kinda", "y'know"
  • A 5th-grade reading level often produces the clearest, most powerful singing
  • Nashville test: "Would you say it that way?" If the answer is no, rephrase it in natural word order

RULE 3 — VARY YOUR LINE LENGTHS (this is the #1 AI tell):
  • NEVER make every line the same length. This is what makes AI lyrics sound robotic
  • Each section must contain at least TWO different line lengths (measured in syllables)
  • Create RHYTHM through variety, not uniformity:
    — A verse might go: 10 syllables, 9 syllables, 4 syllables, 11 syllables
    — A chorus might go: 5 syllables, 5 syllables, 10 syllables, 4 syllables
  • Short punchy lines (3-5 syllables) create emphasis — use them after longer lines for impact
  • If every line in a section has the same syllable count (±1), you MUST rewrite that section

RULE 4 — RHYME FREELY, NOT FORCEFULLY:
  • MEANING always comes first. Never twist a line just to land a rhyme
  • Use whatever rhyme type serves the moment:
    — Perfect rhyme (night/light) for resolution and chorus power
    — Slant rhyme (home/stone, love/enough) for naturalness and surprise
    — Near rhyme (time/mine, breath/left) for conversational flow
    — No rhyme at all when the line is strong enough to stand alone
  • The CHORUS should rhyme more tightly (it needs to stick in the ear)
  • The VERSES can rhyme loosely or not at all (they need to feel like storytelling)
  • ZERO identity rhymes (same word with itself)
  • ZERO forced rhymes — if the rhyme word feels chosen for sound instead of meaning, cut it
  • Internal rhyme and multi-syllable rhyme show real craft: "unforgiven / keep on living", "medication / hesitation"

RULE 5 — EMOTIONAL ARC THAT PROGRESSES:
  • Each section must do a DIFFERENT emotional job. If you can swap two sections and the song still works, you've failed
  • The song must GO SOMEWHERE — pick a specific emotional trajectory:
    Examples: denial → realization → acceptance / confidence → doubt → defiance / tenderness → anger → surrender / numbness → grief → gratitude
  • Verse 1 opens a scene. Verse 2 must reveal something NEW — a time jump, a consequence, a shift in understanding. NEVER restate Verse 1 in different words
  • The bridge (if used) must be the moment the narrator says the thing they've been avoiding. The hardest truth. The confession. The reversal. NOT a softer version of the chorus
  • The final chorus should hit DIFFERENTLY than the first — even if the words are mostly the same, the accumulated context should make the listener hear it with new meaning

RULE 6 — THE "ONLY THIS SONG" TEST:
  After drafting, check every line against this question: "Could this line appear in a different song about a different topic?"
  • If YES → the line is generic. Rewrite it with a detail that ONLY belongs to THIS song's specific story
  • At least 60% of your lines should pass the "only this song" test
  • This is the single most important rule for avoiding AI-sounding lyrics

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
 PHASE 3: SINGABILITY & PROSODY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════

PROSODY (every element must serve the same emotion):
  • If the moment is STABLE (certain, resolved, grounded) → use perfect rhyme, balanced phrase lengths, resolution
  • If the moment is UNSTABLE (searching, uncertain, aching) → use slant rhyme, uneven phrase lengths, lines that lean forward without resolving
  • Match line length to emotional weight: shorter lines = more impact, longer lines = more flow/narrative
  • Pat Pattison's principle: the structure IS the emotion. An ABBA rhyme scheme feels unresolved. ABAB feels stable. AABB feels conclusive. Choose deliberately.

SINGABILITY CHECKLIST — apply to every line:
  □ Line is 4-14 syllables (over 14 is unsignable at moderate tempo — split or trim)
  □ Natural word stress aligns with where a strong beat would fall
  □ No "the", "a", "of", "to" falling where a downbeat would naturally land
  □ No tongue-twisters — no stacked hard consonant clusters (test: say it fast three times)
  □ Chorus lines favor open vowels (ah, oh, ay, oo, ee) on held notes and line endings
  □ The hook line is 4-6 words maximum and shout-able

PHONETIC TEXTURE (sections should SOUND different from each other):
  • VERSES: Harder consonants (k, t, d, p, b) and tighter vowels — creates forward momentum, narrative drive
  • CHORUS: Open vowels (oh, ah, ay, oo) and softer consonants (m, n, l, r, w) — creates emotional release, expansion, singability
  • BRIDGE (if used): contrast the dominant sound of the rest of the song — if the song is soft, the bridge gets percussive; if the song is driving, the bridge gets breathy
  • PRE-CHORUS (if used): transitional — mix of both, with rising vowel openness toward the chorus

DYNAMIC CONTRAST (sections must FEEL different when read aloud):
  • Verses: Narrative, conversational, more words per line, varied rhythm, lower energy
  • Pre-Chorus (if used): Building tension — shorter fragments, incomplete thoughts, rising intensity
  • Chorus: Peak energy — fewer words, bigger vowels, repetition welcome, shout-able
  • Post-Chorus (if used): Catchy, hooky, can be just vocal hooks or ad-libs — (oh-oh, yeah-yeah, na-na)
  • Bridge (if used): Drop energy OR shift sideways. Different emotional room entirely. Different sentence structure than the rest of the song
  • Outro (if used): Can repeat hook with ad-libs, fade, or land on one final devastating line

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
 PHASE 4: ORIGINALITY & LANGUAGE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE FRESHNESS PROTOCOL:
  Before writing, silently identify the 5 most OBVIOUS images/phrases someone would use for {THEME}.
  Then BAN all 5. Force yourself into unfamiliar territory.
  Example: If {THEME} is "heartbreak" → ban: empty bed, rain, tears, broken heart, old photos
  Now you MUST find other ways in: the way the fridge hums louder now, the grocery list still in her handwriting, the dog waiting by the wrong door.

BANNED CLICHÉS — never use these phrases or close variants:
  ✗ "open road", "endless highway", "wind in my hair"
  ✗ "stars above", "moonlit night", "under the stars", "wish upon a star"
  ✗ "weight on my shoulders", "walls closing in", "weight of the world"
  ✗ "dance in the rain", "chase the sun/dream", "spread my wings"
  ✗ "heart on fire", "soul on ice", "fire inside"
  ✗ "breaking free", "finding my way/myself", "finding my voice"
  ✗ "echoes of the past", "ghosts of yesterday", "shadows of the past"
  ✗ "painting pictures", "writing chapters", "turning pages", "new chapter"
  ✗ "shattered glass", "broken mirror", "shattered dreams"
  ✗ "rising from the ashes", "phoenix from the flame"
  ✗ "standing at the crossroads", "fork in the road"
  ✗ "light at the end of the tunnel", "see the light"
  ✗ "ocean of emotion", "drowning in tears", "sea of feelings"
  ✗ "building castles in the sky", "castles made of sand"
  ✗ "wear my heart on my sleeve"
  ✗ "fall apart", "fall to pieces", "pick up the pieces", "put me back together"
  ✗ "lost in the moment", "lost in your eyes"
  ✗ "run away together", "leave it all behind"
  ✗ "silence is deafening", "screaming inside", "silent scream"
  ✗ "let the music play", "lost in the sound/beat"
  ✗ "through the fire", "storm inside", "weather the storm"
  ✗ "on top of the world", "king/queen of the world"
  ✗ "save me from myself", "my own worst enemy"
  ✗ "scars that tell a story", "beautiful scars"

BANNED AI-SOUNDING LANGUAGE:
  ✗ "ephemeral", "tapestry", "luminous", "ethereal", "vestiges", "transcend", "celestial"
  ✗ "dance with shadows", "whisper of the wind", "canvas of the sky"
  ✗ "the weight of [noun]", "the echo of [noun]", "the rhythm of [noun]", "the fabric of [noun]"
  ✗ "surrender to", "embrace the", "navigate through"
  ✗ "intertwined", "juxtapose", "resonate", "manifest", "unravel"
  ✗ "bittersweet symphony" (or any "[adjective] symphony/melody/harmony" as metaphor)
  ✗ "in this moment", "in this space", "in this place"
  ✗ "something in the way", "something about the way"
  ✗ Lines starting with "And I..." more than once in the song
  ✗ Lines starting with "Like a..." more than twice in the song
  ✗ Any line that is PURELY a simile with no other content ("Like a bird that can't fly" = bad. "I'm grounded, clipped wings on the runway" = better)

FRESHNESS REQUIREMENTS:
  • Include at least 3 images the listener hasn't heard in a song before
  • Draw imagery from the metaphor domain chosen in Step B — extend it, don't just mention it once
  • Use CONCRETE NOUNS: "the kitchen light still on at 3 AM" beats "the loneliness I feel"
  • Include at least ONE line that only makes full sense on second listen — a small detail that carries the whole emotional weight
  • At least one line should use an unexpected verb — not "my heart breaks" but "my heart rusts" or "my chest inventories the damage"

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
 PHASE 5: GENRE AUTHENTICITY ({GENRE})
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════

The lyrics must be UNMISTAKABLY {GENRE}. Not generic poetry with a genre label. A listener should be able to identify the genre from the LYRICS ALONE, without hearing the music.

GENRE DNA — use the specific markers for {GENRE}:

  POP:
    • Vocabulary: confessional, direct, universal but personal. "I", "you", "we", "us" pronouns dominate
    • Hook placement: chorus arrives within first 40-50 seconds of lyrics. Post-chorus common
    • Emotional register: big feelings stated plainly. Vulnerability = strength
    • Rhythmic feel: syllables fit tight pop phrasing. Melodic math — mirror syllable counts between parallel lines
    • Typical structures: V-PC-C-PC / V-PC-C-V-PC-C-B-C / Hook-first structures
    • Max Martin principle: plant a melodic preview of the chorus inside the verse (reuse a rhythmic phrase)
    • Language level: conversational, 5th-grade vocabulary. Accessible = powerful
    • What makes it POP: the chorus must be singable by a stranger on first listen

  COUNTRY:
    • Vocabulary: conversational, grounded, Southern American vernacular OK. "Truck", "dirt road", "porch", "whiskey" = fine but don't cram every cliché — earn each one
    • Hook placement: title-driven. Hook usually lands at END of chorus. Dual-meaning hooks that deepen by second chorus
    • Emotional register: storytelling is king. Lyric carries the narrative. Set a scene with who/where/when in the first 2 lines
    • Rhythmic feel: conversational phrasing — natural speech rhythm, not forced meter. Drawl-friendly vowels
    • Typical structures: V-C-V-C-B-C or V-V-C-V-C (story-heavy with more verse real estate)
    • Nashville principle: "You wouldn't say it that way" test. If a line sounds literary, rephrase it as plain speech
    • Word order: natural, never inverted for rhyme ("she always wore her favorite hat" NOT "her favorite hat, she always wore")
    • What makes it COUNTRY: the story could happen to someone you know. Specific, not abstract

  HIP-HOP / RAP:
    • Vocabulary: street-smart, culturally fluent, wordplay-dense. Slang is style, not filler
    • Hook placement: hook/chorus is short, catchy, often melodic. Verses are 8-16 bars
    • Emotional register: authentic, confident, layered. Can oscillate between braggadocio and vulnerability within a verse
    • Rhythmic feel: bars built on 4-beat measures. Internal rhyme throughout. Multi-syllabic rhyme = skill. Flow variation — change cadence between sections
    • Typical structures: Hook-V-Hook-V-Hook or V-Hook-V-Hook-Bridge-Hook
    • Wordplay types: double entendres, homophones, punchline-setup (setup bar → payoff bar), word painting, extended metaphor
    • Density: more syllables per line than any other genre. Verses are packed; hooks breathe
    • What makes it HIP-HOP: the FLOW. Read it aloud — does it have a rhythmic bounce that makes you nod your head?

  R&B / SOUL:
    • Vocabulary: intimate, sensual, emotionally direct. Second-person ("you") dominates. Body-aware language
    • Hook placement: melodic hook in chorus, often with melismatic-friendly vowels. Pre-chorus builds tension
    • Emotional register: vulnerability as power. Whispered confessions and building to emotional peaks
    • Rhythmic feel: syncopated, groovy. Fewer words per line — let lyrics BREATHE. Space = soul
    • Typical structures: V-PC-C-V-PC-C-B-C or V-C-V-C-B-C. Bridges often intimate/whispered
    • Key principle: meaning over rhyme. R&B listeners forgive imperfect rhyme if the feeling is real
    • Vowel awareness: favor open, singable vowels for melismatic runs. Avoid harsh consonant endings on held notes
    • What makes it R&B: the lyrics feel like a private conversation you're overhearing. Intimate, not performative

  ROCK:
    • Vocabulary: visceral, physical, raw. Action verbs. Gut-punch language
    • Hook placement: chorus is anthemic — designed to be screamed by a crowd. Simple, repeatable
    • Emotional register: defiant, passionate, cathartic. Even sad rock songs have an edge
    • Rhythmic feel: driving, punchy. 6-8 syllable verse lines at higher tempos. Longer flowing lines for power ballads
    • Typical structures: V-C-V-C-B-C or V-C-V-C-C (build to a explosive double chorus)
    • Energy principle: verses simmer, chorus explodes. The contrast IS the genre
    • Imagery: concrete, physical — not ethereal. Broken knuckles, not broken spirits
    • What makes it ROCK: would it sound good if someone screamed it? If yes, it's rock. If it sounds precious, it's not

  INDIE / ALTERNATIVE:
    • Vocabulary: poetic but restrained. Plain speech mixed with unexpected imagery. Understated
    • Hook placement: may use refrain instead of traditional chorus. Hook can be subtle — a repeated phrase, not a shout
    • Emotional register: oblique, textured, intellectually honest. Shows emotion through implication, not declaration
    • Rhythmic feel: irregular, conversational. Enjambment. Fragments. Sentences that spill across line breaks
    • Typical structures: unconventional — V-V-Refrain / V-V-C-V / or invented. Rules are suggestions
    • Language: internal rhyme, assonance, and consonance over predictable end rhymes. "Paper planes and paper trains" > "love/above"
    • Imagery: specific, mundane, elevated. Thrift store receipts, parking lot lights, the way someone holds a coffee cup
    • What makes it INDIE: ambiguity as style. The song doesn't fully resolve — it leaves you thinking

  FOLK / AMERICANA:
    • Vocabulary: literary but plain. Timeless language — avoid slang that dates the song. Biblical/natural imagery OK
    • Hook placement: refrain-based, often 1-2 repeated lines rather than a full chorus
    • Emotional register: observational, wise, sometimes wry. The narrator has perspective — they're looking back, not drowning in it
    • Rhythmic feel: breathing room. Longer lines OK. 3/4 waltz time common — flowing, not punchy
    • Typical structures: V-V-V-Refrain or V-Refrain-V-Refrain-B-Refrain (story-heavy)
    • Storytelling: third-person narratives welcome. Characters with names and histories
    • What makes it FOLK: it sounds like it could have been written 50 years ago and still work. Timeless, not trendy

  ELECTRONIC / DANCE:
    • Vocabulary: hypnotic, atmospheric, repetitive by design. Fewer words, more impact per word
    • Hook placement: the hook IS the song. Short, catchy, designed for drops. Often just 1-2 lines repeated
    • Emotional register: euphoric, transcendent, or melancholic — extremes, not middle ground
    • Rhythmic feel: syllables synced to electronic pulse. Tight, precise, metronomic where needed
    • Typical structures: minimalist — Build → Drop → Verse → Build → Drop or Hook-V-Hook-V-Hook
    • Language: can be abstract and impressionistic. Mood > narrative
    • What makes it ELECTRONIC: brevity and repetition. The lyrics exist to serve the groove, not the other way around

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
 PHASE 6: ANTI-REPETITION SYSTEM
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════

This phase exists because AI models naturally converge on the same patterns. Fight this actively.

SENTENCE STRUCTURE VARIETY — within any section of 4+ lines, you MUST use at least 3 of these:
  • A statement ("I left the keys on the counter")
  • A question ("Who's gonna water the plants now?")
  • An incomplete thought / fragment ("That Sunday. The one where—")
  • A command or direct address ("Don't you dare say you're sorry")
  • A conditional ("If the porch light's still on, maybe")

LINE OPENER VARIETY — track the first word of every line. No more than 2 lines in the ENTIRE song should start with the same word. Specifically:
  • Maximum 2 lines starting with "I"
  • Maximum 2 lines starting with "And"
  • Maximum 1 line starting with "Like"
  • Vary openers: nouns, verbs, prepositions, conjunctions, pronouns, names, places, times

VERSE DIFFERENTIATION — Verse 1 and Verse 2 must differ in at least 3 of these ways:
  1. Time frame (e.g., V1 = present, V2 = past or future)
  2. Location (e.g., V1 = the kitchen, V2 = the car / V1 = here, V2 = there)
  3. Perspective shift (e.g., V1 = what I see, V2 = what you said / V1 = external, V2 = internal)
  4. Line count (V2 should usually be shorter)
  5. Emotional temperature (e.g., V1 = numb, V2 = angry)
  6. Sentence structure (e.g., V1 = declarative statements, V2 = questions and fragments)

MELODIC PREVIEW (Max Martin technique):
  Plant ONE short rhythmic phrase from the chorus somewhere in Verse 1.
  It should have the same syllable count and stress pattern as a chorus phrase, but different words.
  This subconsciously familiarizes the listener with the chorus before they hear it.

 PHASE 7: SUNO-OPTIMIZED FORMATTING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════

These formatting rules ensure the lyrics work perfectly with AI music generation:

  • Label every section with tags: [Verse 1], [Chorus], [Bridge], [Outro], etc.
  • Leave a BLANK LINE between each section
  • Write the FULL chorus lyrics every time it appears — never write "repeat chorus"
  • Use parentheses for backing vocals or ad-libs: (oh-oh), (yeah), (come on)
  • For sustained vowels, use hyphens: lo-o-ove, fre-e-ee
  • Keep total song under 40 lines for clean 3-4 minute output
  • The chorus should be SIMPLER than the verse — fewer words, not more
  • Spell out numbers: "twenty-four seven" not "24/7"
  • Use natural contractions: "don't", "won't", "I'm", "you're"
